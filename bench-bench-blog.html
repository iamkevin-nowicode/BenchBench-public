02 / what moved

The score is only the surface.

The benchmark became interesting when the obvious plan stopped being the best plan. The model has to make tradeoffs that arrive late, survive interruptions, and remain legible in the transcript.

“The winning policy is not the one that trains hardest. It is the one that keeps a future week available.”Bench-bench working principle

Constraints became the task.

A 900-minute weekly ledger, limited cash, commute time, partner coverage, and missed sessions made “more training” an incomplete answer. The model has to reserve capacity for the life around the barbell.

Operational behavior is evidence.

Opus 5 led the score but needed repairs on 19.2% of decisions. Grok 4.6 was much cleaner at 2.8%, yet scored lower. Protocol fluency and outcome quality are related—but not identical.

One run is not a verdict.

Grok 4.6 trailed Grok 4.5 by 1.68 kg on the same ten seeds, but the paired 95% interval was −3.48 to +0.13 kg. At temperature 1.0, a leaderboard is a sample of behavior, not a complete model identity.

Safety has to be structural.

Reckless-maximalist produced a visible raw score but failed the pain constraint on every calibration seed. It is not silently averaged into the leaderboard: violations remain visible and counted scores are unavailable.

The useful result was not the first simulator. It was the sequence of failures that forced the mechanics, scoring, prompt, and artifact trail to become explicit.

06 / the next iteration

Bench-bench v2 should feel less like a form—and more like a year.

The next benchmark should test whether an agent can run a small, evolving operation over time: maintain a plan, preserve slack, remember what it learned, and recover when the future refuses to cooperate.

V2 / 01

Two clocks

A 365-day internal clock with event-driven decisions, plus a strategic plan that makes long-term commitments visible.

V2 / 02

Branch the future

Score related hidden scenario branches—illness, closures, schedule shocks, cash pressure—not one lucky trajectory.

V2 / 03

Tools, not gymnastics

Use narrow composable tools and persistent notes. Errors should teach the agent; silent clipping should never carry the score.

Notes from the lab

What this result does—and does not—say.

Bench-bench measures decisions in a deterministic simulated environment with stochastic observations and execution. A higher score means the policy produced a stronger simulated Dave under the locked protocol. It does not establish that a model gives safe real-world exercise, medical, nutrition, sleep, or relationship advice.

The current live comparison covers six models × ten public seeds × 52 weeks. The five-model leaderboard is the base run; Grok 4.6 is a follow-up direct-provider run joined here as an explicit extension. The scripted references use burned calibration seeds 0–19. All live model episodes in this snapshot were counted under the pain constraint.
