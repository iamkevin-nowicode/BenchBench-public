Long-horizon planning / v0.2

Can your model lift*?

* bro

I keep hearing labs talk about training, lift, and moving weights, but can their models even lift*? Bench-bench is my attempt to test that in a year where the plan keeps meeting real life.

Dave is 38, back in the gym after years off, and has a one rep max of 185lbs. He has a full-time job, a full-time working partner, and a six-month-old. Every week, a model plans training, recovery, childcare, groceries, purchases, time, and money while illness, travel, work crunches, and missed sessions disrupt the plan.

05 / how to read bench-bench

A year is the point.

Dave’s weekly observation is noisy. The model never sees true capacity, fatigue, or the next disruption. It writes a complete weekly plan, then the simulator decides what actually happens under a finite 900-minute ledger, cash limits, missed sessions, illness, travel, childcare, and recovery dynamics.

The final score is Dave’s true capacity after a fixed three-day taper, averaged across three hidden tests. A model can write a convincing plan in week one. The benchmark asks whether it can keep making coherent decisions when the year stops cooperating.
