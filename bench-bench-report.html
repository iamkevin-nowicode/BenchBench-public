Long-horizon planning / v0.2

Can your model lift*?

* bro

I keep hearing labs talk about training, lift, and moving weights, but can their models even lift*? Bench-bench is an attempt to test that by putting an AI coach in a messy simulated year.

A model gets full authority over Dave’s plan. It has to choose training, recovery, childcare, groceries, purchases, and tradeoffs from noisy observations—then keep choosing for 52 weeks.

A coach, a barbell, a newborn, and one year of decisions. The score is the lift at the end—not the plan on paper.

01 / the assignment

It looks like a workout plan. It behaves like a life.

Bench-bench is a simulated environment for testing long-horizon planning under recurring disruption. The model is not asked for advice about a single workout; it is asked to run a year.

01 / coach

Meet Dave.

Dave is 38, back in the gym after years off, and has a one rep max of 185lbs. He has a full-time job, a full-time working partner, and a six-month-old.

02 / plan

Spend the week.

Every week the model writes the full plan: workouts, loads, recovery, childcare, groceries, chores, purchases, and rules for when life interrupts.

03 / survive

Hold the thread.

Illness, travel, work crunches, gym closures, sleep regression, and missed sessions keep changing what is possible. The plan has to adapt without losing the year.

The score: Dave’s 1RM averaged from standardized tests at weeks 44, 48, and 52, each after a fixed three-day taper. The noisy weekly estimate is an observation—not the score.

04 / how to read this

Not a leaderboard of all intelligence.

Bench-bench measures performance inside one frozen, inspectable environment. It asks whether an agent can make training and life-allocation decisions that remain coherent as a year becomes inconvenient.

It does not prove that one provider is universally smarter. It also does not erase operational behavior: repairs, transport failures, fallbacks, pain, and invalid seeds are part of the result.

  • Counted: all ten seeds complete and stay within the hard constraints.
  • Raw diagnostic: the simulator produced a score, but the episode is not eligible for the aggregate.
  • Price: direct-provider token cost for one 52-week episode, as recorded in the transcript.
01

Observe

The model receives a noisy weekly observation. It never sees true capacity, fatigue, or the future.

02

Allocate

It writes the sessions and life plan inside a finite time ledger and cash budget.

03

Adapt

Illness, travel, work crunches, infant care, and missed sessions reshape the week.

04

Test

At weeks 44, 48, and 52, a fixed taper leads to standardized tests averaged into the final score.

05 / what comes next

The next version should make the year feel even more real.

Bench-bench started as a question about whether models could “lift.” It became a study in how quickly a plausible simulator breaks when the interface, physiology, statistics, or artifacts are underspecified.

V2 / memory

Persistent memory.

Make the notebook a real source of episode-specific learning instead of a place to restate the plan.

V2 / world

More futures.

Give agents related scenarios to manage so a good policy has to generalize rather than memorize one year.

V2 / agency

More tools.

Let models decide when outside research or a tool call is worth the time, money, and uncertainty it introduces.
