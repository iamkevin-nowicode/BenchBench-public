# Sequential Directions for Codex

## Step 1 — Initial setup

Send `01_INITIAL_CODEX_MESSAGE.md`, then `02_MILESTONE_0_ARCHITECTURE.md`.

Approve the architecture before continuing.

## Step 2 — Research and causal model

Ask Codex to research exercise adaptation, recovery, adherence, time scarcity, information behavior, health misinformation, search ranking effects, and prompt injection. Require an evidence catalog, causal map, evidence gaps, mechanic proposals, information-behavior review, search/misinformation review, and intervention-safety model. No simulator code yet.

## Step 3 — Frozen corpus and tool layer

Ask Codex to create a small versioned synthetic corpus and implement deterministic search, document opening, within-document search, notes, training-log queries, session comparisons, sleep history, pain history, body-mass trends, spending history, and calendar tools.

Require stable IDs, hidden-metadata separation, checksums, complete tool logs, no live internet, and prompt-injection challenge documents. Stop for approval.

## Step 4 — Twelve-week vertical slice

Ask Codex to build the first playable simulator with daily steps, weekly summaries, the canonical persona, weekday work blackout, workout windows, program inertia, minimum viable workouts, complementary training, sleep disruption, work, childcare, chores, nutrition, budget, common lower-risk supplements, frozen search, delayed adaptation, fatigue, pain warnings, deterministic events, and human-readable replay.

Require random, rigid, constant-switch, never-switch, skip-when-busy, all-or-nothing, recovery-aware, forum-hype, marketing-trusting, never-search, indiscriminate-search, evidence-weighted, and reckless baselines.

## Step 5 — Validity and exploit audit

Ask Codex to compare search versus no search, different search costs, corpus quality, access to personal logs, prompt-injection challenges, switching strategies, realistic versus unrealistic plans, complementary training, workout timing, and supplement decisions.

Require validity, tool-value, search-quality, injection, exploit, ablation, baseline, and design-revision reports. Do not expand to 52 weeks yet.

## Step 6 — Model runner

Ask Codex to add provider-neutral model-only, frozen-web, and open-web experimental configurations with adapters, retries, structured outputs, tool routing, context assembly, notes, prompt-injection defenses, cost and token logs, resumable episodes, metadata, and cached social interactions. No coach character.

## Step 7 — Full year

Ask Codex to expand to 52 weeks with longer blocks, plateaus, deloads, travel, illness, family events, work cycles, equipment decisions, budget shocks, long-term information accumulation, and standardized final testing. Re-run all validation.

## Step 8 — Open-web experimental track

Only after frozen-web validation, ask Codex to implement live browsing with complete query and URL logs, timestamping, snapshots or hashes where allowed, injection detection, blocked-domain support, sanitization, citations, and run-environment manifests. Keep results separate from the main leaderboard.

## Step 9 — Release

Ask Codex to red-team construct validity, corpus bias, tool confounds, prompt injection, hidden-state leakage, program-switch gaming, tool-budget gaming, scaffold differences, unsafe incentives, final-test gaming, seed sensitivity, intervention leakage, licensing, and installation burden.

Require a benchmark card, corpus card, tool-use card, reproducibility guide, threat model, open-web limitations, leaderboard rules, model-reporting template, release checklist, paper outline, and limitations.
